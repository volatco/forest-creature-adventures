Use and adapt these blocks for GoFundMe, Kickstarter, Patreon, Ko-fi, or direct sponsor pages.

## 1) One-Line Pitch
Forest Creature Adventures is a story-driven technical series where woodland hunter-gatherers learn Volatco + aeonForth workflows to build understandable, shareable computing tools.

## 2) Short Pitch (100-140 words)
Forest Creature Adventures is a comic and animation series about four woodland friends learning practical computing together. Through Volatco and aeonForth, they move from fragile setup problems to reliable workflows for real Forth programs. Each episode teaches both technical skill and human skill: repeatability, clear communication, cooperation, and trust.

This project is for people who want technology that can be understood, repaired, and shared instead of treated as opaque magic. Your support funds the next production block of episodes, including script development, panel generation, visual assembly, and transparent release notes.

Help us build a series where learning is practical, moral, and collaborative.

## 3) Long Campaign Story
We are building Forest Creature Adventures as both a narrative world and a practical learning project.

In this world, the stakes are real: when systems are fragile, the characters lose daylight, fail field plans, and become dependent on external systems they cannot interpret or fix. Their answer is to learn together, document clearly, and build local understanding through Volatco and aeonForth.

The first production arc is about foundations:
- Episode 001: commissioning a stable polyForth path on Volatco B
- Episode 002: reliable debug and reset discipline
- Episode 003: low-power sensing and event-driven behavior
- Episode 004: long-duration reliability and watchdog recovery

Your support funds this work from script to finished episode assets. We publish progress transparently and prioritize practical educational value, continuity, and consistency.

If you believe technical literacy should be collaborative, understandable, and grounded in real workflows, this series is for you.

## 4) Why This Matters
- It makes technical learning approachable without dumbing it down.
- It models cooperation as a technical advantage, not a side message.
- It promotes autonomy: understand, repair, and share your systems.
- It connects storytelling with real engineering practices.
